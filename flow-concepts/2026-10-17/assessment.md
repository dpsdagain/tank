## Verdict

**Yes, this art direction can be used in the present game. The scenery is directly compatible with the background layer. The explosion needs compositing/asset preparation before replacing production VFX.**

## What was inspected

- Concept and scenery: 1376 × 768 RGB JPEG, despite no extension being supplied by the source asset endpoint.
- Explosion sheet: 1024 × 1024 RGB JPEG; 16 equal nominal cells of 256 × 256.
- Bright-pixel bounds were measured per cell at intensity > 40; the measurements are in `asset-inspection.json`. Frame 8 reaches local y = 255, so clipping/bleed requires review. Smoke centroids rise during dissipation; this is not by itself proof of an anchor error, but the sequence should be previewed with a fixed origin.
- Final cell is dominated by the visible generated mark. The compatibility preview therefore reads the first 15 cells, preserving the original source sheet unchanged.
- The supplied file sizes are approximately 153 KB for the concept, 62 KB for scenery, and 112 KB for the VFX sheet. Their dimensions and compressed sizes are practical for this browser game; decoding and real-device frame rate still need checking in any production integration.

## Recommended production approach

1. Adopt the painted scenery as the background once a final marked/unmarked export has been selected. Keep the real-time ground, tank positions, craters and gameplay HUD separate.
2. Use the generated fireball as an additional fire/glow layer, with screen/additive compositing where appropriate. Keep the current particle sparks, dirt, collision events, shockwaves and actual terrain damage.
3. For the dark plume, obtain or prepare a genuine transparent RGBA atlas. Keep RGB color and alpha edges clean so smoke darkens and covers the scene correctly. Request clean gutters and a final empty frame; inspect cell alignment and clipping.
4. Adapt the original `WeaponEffects.sprite()` path to a 4-column, 256 px-cell atlas, with roughly 18–24 FPS for an initial preview. Adjust timing to fit the weapon's impact; do not apply the same orange fireball to every weapon.
5. Use separate explosion families for normal shells, nukes, plasma, lasers, lightning and dirt. This one sheet is a visual test, not a complete replacement for all 36 weapon behaviors.
